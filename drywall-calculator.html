How this is calculated

Drywall installation requires mapping flat panels onto your room's vertical and horizontal surfaces. The calculator figures your total drywall area: Total Surface Area = Wall Area + Ceiling Area. Drywall is estimated using standard 4×8 ft sheets (32 sq ft each), with 10% extra added for cuts around outlets and corners, then rounded up. Joint compound is estimated at one 5-gallon bucket per 500 sq ft for a standard 3-coat finish.

FAQ

Should I buy 4×8 or 4×12 drywall sheets?
For most DIY residential projects with standard 8-foot ceilings, 4×8 sheets are the easiest to transport, handle, and install. If you have long walls or 9-foot ceilings, 4×12 sheets are recommended because they cover more area at once, reducing the number of vertical seams you have to tape and mud.

How much joint compound do I need per sheet?
As a general rule, you'll need approximately 0.05 gallons of ready-mixed joint compound per square foot of drywall. For a standard 4×8 sheet (32 sq ft), expect to use about 1.5 to 2 gallons across all three phases: embedding the tape, the fill coat, and the final skim coat.

How many drywall screws do I need per sheet?
For a standard 4×8 sheet installed on studs spaced 16 inches apart, you'll need approximately 30 to 32 screws, spaced 12 inches apart on both walls and ceilings to prevent sagging panels.
